Clinical and environmental data

Exposure before lung disease diagnosis

Linking clinical timelines to nearby monitoring records to study temporal exposure patterns.

Role
Cohort linkage and temporal analysis
Time window
Two years before diagnosis
Methods
Geospatial matching and clustering
Status
Exploratory research

The question

How do air-pollution patterns vary during the two years before a hypersensitivity pneumonitis diagnosis, and how are those patterns distributed across fibrotic and non-fibrotic groups?

My contribution

I linked clinical records to environmental monitoring data using geographic proximity and diagnosis-relative time windows, then characterized exposure with local temporal patterns and exploratory clustering.

Analysis workflow

  1. Define a two-year exposure window ending at each patient’s diagnosis.
  2. Identify nearby monitors and assess temporal coverage.
  3. Retrieve eligible patterns from the exposure library.
  4. Summarize temporal behavior and compare clusters across the clinical groups.

Why the linkage matters

A monitor must be relevant in both location and time. This workflow makes the exposure window explicit and preserves the connection between each patient timeline and the available environmental record.

Interpretation

This is an exploratory observational analysis. Patterns and cluster differences do not establish causation or validated individual risk prediction. Exposure estimates also depend on monitoring coverage and the relationship between monitor location and personal exposure.
